Our story

It started with a notebook.

Before Mokimo there was an exercise book, filled with machines that hadn't been built yet. The drawings were wrong in every proportion—but the habit was right: decide what the thing must do, draw how it will do it, then go and build it.

Chapters

From exercise book to engineering company.

01 Circa childhood

The notebook

Rockets, cranes, contraptions with far too many moving parts. Nothing in it would have worked—the fins were in the wrong place and the materials didn't exist. But every page asked the same question a real brief asks: what should this do, and how? That question never went away.

02 The training

Learning the trade

Mechanical Engineering at Loughborough, picking up electronics and firmware along the way—because real problems don't respect discipline boundaries. Then three years designing scientific instruments, medical devices, and precision consumer products: places where a drawing error becomes an expensive lesson, and the assembled system always reveals what the parts hid.

03 The foundry

Mokimo, Sheffield

Mokimo was founded to keep the early habit intact at professional scale. Small, so decisions stay close to the hardware—and close to you. We build what we design and test what we build, carrying work through design, build, integration and test. The projects got harder. The notebook logic didn't change.

Principles

What carried through.

01

Draw it first

Every project starts as a drawing that has to justify itself before anything is cut. Cheap on paper, expensive in metal.

02

Build what you design

We keep the work through design, build, integration and test. The gap between paper and product is where projects fail—we don't outsource it.

03

Test what you build

A design isn't finished when it looks right. It's finished when it has been measured, exercised, and proven against what it promised.

The drawings got better. The habit didn't change.

If you have something that needs to exist—a product, a piece of equipment, a production step that needs to work—we'd like to hear about it.
