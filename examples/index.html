Mechanical design · For a university engineering department

Strain-wave gear demonstrator

Problem

A university engineering department needed a physical demonstrator to teach strain-wave ("harmonic") gear reduction — a mechanism that's usually explained with diagrams and is hard to build real intuition for on paper.

Approach

Designed and 3D-printed a working strain-wave gearbox with a visible flex-spline and a dial showing the reduction ratio in real time, then paired it with a simple motor and driver so students could see the mechanism turning under load rather than just reading about it.

Outcome

Delivered a working demonstrator with a 53.5:1 reduction ratio, built end-to-end from design through to the finished model — later reused as a reference point for other compact drivetrain work.

Mechanical, electronic & firmware · Personal project

Bikestow: an automated storage mechanism

Problem

A cramped storage space made for a good excuse to explore what a small, fully custom automation project looks like end-to-end — mechanism, electronics, and control, not just one discipline in isolation.

Approach

Built a CNC gantry mechanism from machined aluminium brackets and lead-screw hardware, designed a custom controller PCB to drive the motors, and hand-assembled and soldered the electronics before wiring the system together for testing.

Outcome

A working prototype that moved from sketch to a functioning automated mechanism — covering mechanical design and fabrication, PCB design, and the firmware to tie it together. The kind of whole-system project Mokimo is built around.
