Strain-wave gear demonstrator

Teaching rig for a university engineering department

Strain-wave — or "harmonic" — gearing is usually taught with diagrams, which makes it difficult to build any real intuition for how the flex-spline actually produces its reduction. This is the same mechanism, made large enough and slow enough to watch.

Mechanical Electronics

The brief

A university engineering department needed a physical demonstrator for teaching strain-wave gear reduction: something a room full of students could gather round, turn by hand or under power, and immediately see the relationship between input and output.

Approach

Designed and 3D-printed a working strain-wave gearbox with a deliberately visible flex-spline, paired with a dial that reads out the reduction in real time. Being able to print the parts meant the geometry could be iterated cheaply until the mechanism ran cleanly and looked legible in motion — which, for a teaching aid, matters as much as the engineering underneath it.

Outcome

A working demonstrator with a 53.5:1 reduction ratio, taken end to end from design through to a finished, usable model, and paired with a motor and driver so it can be run under power rather than only turned by hand.
