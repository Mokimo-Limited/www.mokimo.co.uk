Strain-wave gear demonstrator

University teaching project

A working 53.5:1 strain-wave gear demonstrator with an exposed flex-spline and live reduction dial, engineered as a physical teaching aid.

Mechanical Electronics

Objective

Provide a clear, physical demonstration of strain-wave (harmonic) gearing for engineering students, making the continuous deformation of the flex-spline and the resulting speed reduction visible in real time.

Approach

The gearbox was designed with an open housing and high-contrast components to highlight internal motion. A coaxial dial indicates output rotation relative to the input shaft. Additive manufacturing enabled rapid iteration of tooth profiles and flex-spline geometry before integrating motor drive electronics.

Outcome

A self-contained, motor-driven teaching model operating at a 53.5:1 reduction ratio, allowing immediate observation of flex-spline mechanics under power.
