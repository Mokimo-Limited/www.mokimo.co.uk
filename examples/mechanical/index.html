Mechanical engineering

Mechanisms, structures, and production-ready mechanical design

Design for manufacture & scaling

We've designed parts across the full range, from one-offs and prototype runs of 30–50, through to production volumes of 20,000 units — including injection-moulded parts (viable from quantities as low as ~50 off), where the design considerations differ markedly from 3D printing or machining, but which open up options the other processes simply can't match. That includes producing early prototypes of moulded parts to de-risk the design before committing to the cost of tooling, and specifying the jigs, fixtures, and production drawings that assembly and external fab houses need to build against consistently at volume.

Beyond the obvious

"Mechanical" often brings to mind mechanisms, hinges, and linkages — and we're glad to help there — but the discipline covers a lot more ground:

  • Fluidics, pneumatics & sealing
  • Sub-compact design & ingress protection — adhesive/bonded/sonic-welded assemblies, fastening of glass and ceramics
  • Materials selection — balancing cost, weight, manufacturability, thermal stability, aesthetics, and corrosion resistance, including biocompatibility for science and medical applications
  • Surface finishes — media blasting, anodising, passivation, brushed metal, paint
  • Design around motors, bearings & gears — the interface between mechanical and electronics
  • Sheet-metal part design
  • Engineering drawings for parts and assemblies, including GD&T to ASME Y14.5
  • ECAD–MCAD integration — including fitting multi-board flex-rigid PCBs into tight assemblies to simplify build and improve compactness
  • Supplier sourcing for moulding, machining & prototyping

Professional experience, 2021 · Public case study ↗

Energy-saving plug socket: prototype to production

Problem

An energy-saving plug socket needed to go from working prototype to a manufacturable, compliant product, built at a production volume of roughly 20,000 units and fully meeting BS1363-2 — the UK standard governing plugs and socket-outlets.

Approach

As primary mechanical engineer, worked closely with the electronics team to make sure everything fit, while going through BS1363-2 line by line to confirm the design met it. Designed the injection-moulded housing — including material selection to meet UL94 flammability requirements, strength, impact, and water-absorption testing — a sliding shutter safety mechanism, and the sheet-metal parts, including custom beryllium-copper electrical contacts for the line/neutral pins engineered against requirements for insertion force, contact resistance, and lifetime durability.

Outcome

A compliant, production-ready design manufactured at ~20,000-unit scale. Full write-up on the EG Technology case study page ↗.

For a university engineering department

Strain-wave gear demonstrator

Problem

A university engineering department needed a physical demonstrator to teach strain-wave ("harmonic") gear reduction — a mechanism that's usually explained with diagrams and is hard to build real intuition for on paper.

Approach

Designed and 3D-printed a working strain-wave gearbox with a visible flex-spline and a dial showing the reduction ratio in real time, then paired it with a motor and driver so students could see the mechanism turning under load.

Outcome

Delivered a working demonstrator with a 53.5:1 reduction ratio, built end-to-end from design through to the finished model.
